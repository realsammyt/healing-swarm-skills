# Literacy Audit Template

> Template for the periodic consciousness-literacy check-in produced by the
> `/consciousness-audit` skill. Reviews a stretch of practice history and records
> a self-rated literacy level for each of the four clusters in
> `shared/consciousness-literacy-levels.md`, with the evidence behind each
> reading. It is a reflective self-assessment, never a clinical measurement, and
> "not enough practice history to assess" is a complete answer.

## What This Audit Is For

[2-3 sentences. This audit is a snapshot of how skilled you currently feel at
noticing, entering, sustaining, and leaving your own states, tracked separately
in four clusters. It is a mirror on your practice history, not a grade, and a
cluster you have barely practiced is expected to read as unassessed.]

> **Evidence note:** The four-level literacy taxonomy is a self-report
> reflection tool built on Carl Hayden Smith's analogy that practices build
> consciousness literacy the way letters and words build reading literacy. It is
> a theoretical frame, not a validated instrument. The levels have no published
> norms, no cutoff scores, and no diagnostic meaning. Research suggests that
> sustained practice changes what a person can notice and do; these levels
> record your own honest reading of that, and nothing more.

> **A note on boundaries:** This is a private reflection, not a clinical or
> diagnostic tool. It measures nothing about your health or your worth, and it
> does not replace medical or psychological care. No level here predicts any
> health outcome.

## How to Read Yourself Against the Levels

The same four ordered labels apply inside every cluster: **Nascent**,
**Developing**, **Established**, **Embodied**.

1. Rate each cluster separately, against the observable markers in
   `shared/consciousness-literacy-levels.md`, not against a felt sense of how
   advanced you are.
2. Ask "what can I notice and do", never "how special do I feel".
3. Anchor each rating to something that actually happened in the period above.
   If you cannot point to an instance, you cannot point to a level.
4. **When two levels both seem plausible, take the lower one.** Uncertainty
   steps down, never up.
5. Uneven levels across clusters are expected. Established in one and nascent in
   another, in the same week, is ordinary.
6. A level that moved backward is data, not failure.

## Support

Most audits need nothing from this section. It is here because a periodic review
is a reasonable place to notice a pattern that practice alone should not carry.

**Pause the practice and seek support if the period shows any of these:**

- Sleep getting persistently worse after practice sessions
- Feeling unreal, detached from your body, or unable to reorient after practice
- Anxiety or emotional flooding that does not settle within a session
- Practice becoming compulsive, or distress at the idea of skipping it
- Beliefs about being chosen, uniquely able, or able to read others' minds
- Any measure worsening consistently across four or more weeks
- Thoughts of harming yourself

None of these is a level. They are a signal to stop rating and start reaching
out. Full protocols are in `shared/crisis-response.md`.

> **Crisis resources (US defaults; use your own region's equivalents):**
>
> - **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** — call or text **988**
> - **Crisis Text Line** — text **HOME** to **741741**
> - For a medical emergency, call your local emergency number (911 in the US)
>
> Reaching out is not a failure of practice. It is the right next step, and it
> is what this audit is for when the pattern points that way.

nobody's finish line, and an honest partial audit is more useful than a rushed

## Element Guidelines

### Header Block and Practices Reviewed

**Purpose:** Ground the audit in a specific period and a specific practice
history, so the levels have something to be read against

**Key principles:**

- Period covered is explicit (start, end, approximate number of weeks)
- Practices are listed with their cluster and rough frequency, not exact counts
- Gaps, pauses, and disruptions are recorded as ordinary context, not as
  failures to explain away
- Prior audit date recorded, or "none, this is the first"

### Cluster Sections

**Purpose:** Rate each cluster separately against the canonical taxonomy

**Required:**

- Exactly the four cluster names from
  `shared/consciousness-literacy-levels.md`: Somatic, Perceptual, Relational,
  Attentional. Do not invent, rename, merge, or add clusters.
- Exactly the four level labels: Nascent, Developing, Established, Embodied.
  Do not invent intermediate levels and do not add a level above embodied.
- Each cluster carries its own evidence blockquote, drawn from the practice
  history in the header, describing a behaviour rather than a feeling
- "What moving one level would look like" is drawn from the graduation markers
  in the shared resource, not improvised
- An honest-uncertainty slot on every cluster, where "not enough practice
  history to assess" is an explicit, complete, unpunished entry

### The Step-Down Rule

**Purpose:** Keep self-rating conservative

**Required:**

- Stated in "How to Read Yourself Against the Levels" and repeated in the
  uncertainty slot: when two levels both seem plausible, record the lower one
- Never resolve ambiguity upward, and never encourage a person toward a level
  they are unsure of

### Trends Section

**Purpose:** Show a trend per cluster over weeks and months

**Required:**

- Comparison to the prior audit, per cluster, with a context column
- An explicit "first audit, baseline only" path when there is no prior audit
- No combined score, no literacy number, no summing or averaging across clusters
- Movement down framed as ordinary data with context, never as loss or failure
- No causal language linking any practice to any level change

### Support Section

**Purpose:** Catch a destabilization pattern that a level rating would miss

**Required:**

- Concrete, observable signs (sleep, disorientation, flooding, compulsion,
  grandiosity, sustained worsening, thoughts of self-harm)
- An explicit statement that these are not levels and stop the rating exercise
- Pointer to `shared/crisis-response.md`
- 988 (call or text), text HOME to 741741, and "call your local emergency
  number (911 in the US)" all present, with the US-default caveat stated
- Reaching out framed as the right next step, never as a failed practice

### Evidence Framing

**Purpose:** Keep claims honest

**Required:**

- Smith's reading-literacy analogy attributed to Carl Hayden Smith and labeled
  as a theoretical frame
- The taxonomy labeled a self-report tool, not a validated instrument, with no
  norms, cutoffs, or diagnostic meaning
- "Research suggests" language, no "science proves", per
  `shared/evidence-language.md`
- No outcome promise anywhere: no level predicts a health result
- Medical and psychological disclaimer present

## Accessibility Guidelines

### Physical Accessibility

- No writing required; every entry can be spoken aloud or held in mind
- Usable in any position or setting
- No equipment needed

### Sensory Accessibility

- Screen-reader friendly tables with short cells
- A spoken-response alternative for every checkbox and row
- Plain-language cluster descriptions alongside the cluster names

### Cognitive Accessibility

- One cluster per section, four plain options plus an unassessed option
- Predictable structure repeated identically across all four clusters
- No requirement to complete every cluster in one sitting, or at all
- Short prompts, no scoring arithmetic anywhere

### Psychological Safety

- "Not enough practice history to assess" offered as a real answer, not a gap
- No cluster framed as the important one, and no ranking between people
- Movement backward presented as ordinary data with context
- Explicit permission to stop the audit and seek support instead
- Not a clinical assessment and not therapy
