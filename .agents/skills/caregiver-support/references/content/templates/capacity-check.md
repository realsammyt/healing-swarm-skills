# Capacity Check Template

> Template for caregiver support artifacts: an honest snapshot of current
> capacity, a boundary worksheet, a respite micro-practice menu sized to a
> real day, and clear when-to-seek-support indicators.

---

## Structure

```markdown
# Capacity Check: [Situation in the caregiver's own words]

**Who you are caring for:** [Relationship, in plain words]
**How long:** [Duration of caregiving so far]
**Date of this snapshot:** [Date]

---

## First, the plain part

This is a description of where you are right now. It is not a test, it does
not produce a score, and a low reading is not evidence about you. Low
capacity is the ordinary result of sustained caregiving.

*This is companionship and practical support, not therapy. It does not
replace medical or mental health care. If you want a formal assessment of
caregiver burden, a clinician, social worker, or caregiver support program
can offer one.*

**Notes on holding these**

- With cognitive impairment, a boundary is held through structure and
  redirection rather than through agreement. The other person may not be
  able to remember or accept it. It still holds.
- You do not owe anyone a justification. "This is what I have left" is a

- A boundary with yourself counts: not answering after a certain hour,

## When to Reach for More Support

Caregiver burnout and compassion fatigue are real, well documented, and
worth getting help for. They are not weakness and not a failure of love.

**Worth a call this week if:**

- [ ] Sleep has been broken for weeks with no way to recover it inside the
      current arrangement
- [ ] You feel nothing at all, where there used to be something
- [ ] You are drinking or taking more than you meant to, more often
- [ ] Your own health problems are going unattended because there is no time
- [ ] There is no help and no break, and no plan for either
- [ ] You are frightened of what you might do at the end of your patience

**Reach out now if:**

- You are having thoughts of harming yourself
- You are having thoughts of harming the person you care for

> Having a thought like that does not make you dangerous or a bad person. It
> is a signal that the load is past what one person can carry, and it needs
> another set of hands today.
>
> - **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** call or text **988** (US)
> - **Crisis Text Line:** text **HOME** to **741741** (US)
> - **If anyone is in immediate danger:** call your local emergency number
>   (911 in the US)
> - **Outside the US:** https://findahelpline.com

**Ordinary routes people often do not know exist**

| Where | What they may offer |
|-------|---------------------|
| The social worker attached to the hospital, clinic, or hospice | Respite options, benefits, family meetings. Most families never call |
| Area Agency on Aging (US) | Local respite programs, adult day services, referrals |
| The disease-specific organization | Caregiver helplines, support groups, practical guides |
| Your own primary care provider | An appointment about you, not about them |
| A therapist who works with caregivers | Ongoing support that is not one more thing to manage |
| Peer support, local or online | People who do not need the situation explained |

*Availability, cost, and eligibility vary. Each of these is one phone call
to find out.*
```

## Element Guidelines

### The Snapshot

**Purpose:** Give the caregiver an honest picture of the ground they are
standing on, so that what gets offered next actually fits.

**Requirements:**

- NEVER produce a total, score, level, grade, or stage
- NEVER imply that a low reading means the caregiver is failing
- Sleep and support come first; they determine what is possible
- "Zero unbroken minutes" must be a valid, unremarkable answer
- Body signals are observations, never diagnoses
- "The Unsaid" line is optional and never prompted twice

**On measurement instruments:** validated caregiver-burden instruments exist,
the Zarit Burden Interview being the most widely used in research and
clinical settings. It may be **mentioned** as something a clinician or
support program might use. It is never administered, reproduced, or scored
in this artifact, and no substitute score is invented.

### The Boundary Worksheet

**Purpose:** Turn "I need better boundaries" into three sentences the person
can actually say.

**Requirements:**

- One boundary per block, specific rather than principled
- The exact sentence, short enough to be remembered under pressure
- The cost line is mandatory. A boundary that costs nothing is a wish
- A plan for non-compliance, because in asymmetric care the boundary is
  often held unilaterally
- Boundaries with the family and boundaries with oneself both count
- Never require the care recipient's agreement for the boundary to be valid

### The Respite Menu

**Purpose:** Hand over something that fits inside an interrupted day.

**Requirements:**

- Every practice interruptible without loss
- Every practice mapped to a specific moment in THIS person's day, not to a
  generic day
- Range from 30 seconds to 10 minutes; the short end is not a consolation
  version
- No streaks, no tracking, no daily requirement
- Never framed as something the caregiver "owes themselves"
- Screened against the contraindications reference before inclusion

### When to Reach for More Support

**Purpose:** Name the edge of what a practice can do, and make the next step
concrete.

**Requirements:**

- Burnout and compassion fatigue named as real and support-worthy
- Harm-to-self and harm-to-care-recipient indicators BOTH present, handled
  without judgment, and never softened out of existence
- Crisis resources stated exactly: 988 (call or text), text HOME to 741741,
  and "call your local emergency number (911 in the US)"
- Ordinary routes listed, since most caregivers do not know a hospital
  social worker is available to them
- No guarantees about availability, cost, or eligibility

## Language Guidelines

| Use these | Avoid these |
|-----------|-------------|
| "Many caregivers describe exactly this" | "That's a symptom of caregiver burnout" |
| "That is an arithmetic problem, not a character problem" | "You're being too hard on yourself" |
| "What is the smallest thing that would help?" | "Make time for yourself" |
| "You are allowed to want this to be over" | "Focus on the time you have left" |
| "You do not have to be fine about this" | "You're so strong" |
| "One phone call to find out" | "They'll definitely be able to help" |

**Never appears in this artifact:**

- "You can't pour from an empty cup"
- "At least you'll have no regrets"
- "They're lucky to have you"
- "Everything happens for a reason"
- Any stage model for grief presented as prescriptive
- Any timeline for grief, anticipatory or otherwise
- Any diagnostic language applied to the caregiver or the care recipient

---

## Accessibility Guidelines

### Practical Accessibility

- Readable on a phone at 2am, useful in the first three lines
- No section requires completing a previous section
- Every practice works one-handed, standing, or seated
- Checkbox lists usable without writing anything down

### Cognitive Accessibility

- Short sentences, one idea each
- No section longer than a phone screen
- The crisis block is self-contained and never requires context above it

### Emotional Accessibility

- Nothing is scored, ranked, or graded
- No section implies a right answer
- Skipping any section is explicitly fine
- Guilt, resentment, and relief are named as ordinary before they are
  discussed

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing a capacity check:

- [ ] Snapshot describes, never scores
- [ ] Sleep and support captured first
- [ ] "Zero unbroken minutes" treated as a normal answer
- [ ] Body signals framed as observations, not diagnoses
- [ ] No burden instrument administered, reproduced, or scored
- [ ] At least one boundary block includes an exact sentence
- [ ] Every boundary names its cost
- [ ] Every boundary has a plan for non-compliance
- [ ] No practice requires the care recipient's participation
- [ ] Respite menu mapped to this person's actual day
- [ ] Every respite practice interruptible, equipment-free, privacy-free
- [ ] Practices screened against the contraindications reference
- [ ] No streaks, tracking, or daily requirements anywhere
- [ ] Burnout and compassion fatigue named as real and support-worthy
- [ ] Harm-to-self AND harm-to-care-recipient indicators both present
- [ ] Crisis resources exact: 988, HOME to 741741, local emergency number
- [ ] Ordinary support routes listed without guarantees
- [ ] Cross-references to grief-healing and community-healing present
- [ ] "Not therapy" stated plainly at the top and the bottom
- [ ] No stage models, no grief timelines
- [ ] No pathologizing of guilt, resentment, or relief
- [ ] Tone is practical and warm, with no inspiration
